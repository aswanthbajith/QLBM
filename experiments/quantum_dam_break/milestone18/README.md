# Milestone 18 — audited three-dimensional link transport and deterministic dissipation

## Scope and decision
Milestone 17 demonstrated matched-density CH coupling to one shear mode. A new remote branch (`research/quantum-multiphase-benchmarks`, `0f749a472458c5a354da0e6f5b94f88a71868ad4`) appeared since that milestone. Source review exposed a more immediate correctness issue than extending the Galerkin momentum model: the branch's coupled D3Q19/D3Q7 streaming overwrites populations on solid cells, instead of bouncing fluid links back to the sending fluid cell. Its `DeterministicDissipationChannelD3Q19.apply` also uses classical matrix multiplication/scalar relaxation, and the coupled solver uses classical collisions. We therefore prioritize boundary correctness and an executable dissipative primitive. Neither milestone 17 nor the remote source is overwritten.

## Transport
For a fluid source x with velocity c_i, the correct closed-link map is `(x,i) -> (x+c_i,i)` if the destination is fluid and `(x,opp(i))` otherwise. Periodic wrapping is configurable. Solid and padded basis codes are fixed; callers must initialize solid populations to zero. The map factors into two involutions: swap each unblocked directed link with its reverse counterpart at the other endpoint, then reverse velocity at every fluid cell. Both are compiled using Gray-path, fully controlled X gates. The resulting permutation is a unitary in amplitude encoding (including signed or complex amplitudes). Direct cycle compilation is also implemented and cheaper for this particular geometry. It is a different gate decomposition of the same map. The code exports optional Qiskit circuits; no Qiskit is installed here. Counts are fully-controlled X gates, not native transpiled costs. The classical preprocessing is explicit in geometry compilation. Diagonal links use endpoint halfway bounce-back, not a continuous-surface intersection model.

## Two distinct trace-preserving contractions
Branch model: one rail qubit, `rho=diag((1+d/B)/2,(1-d/B)/2)`, `RY` on an environment, environment-controlled X on the rail, and trace out the environment. The population expectation scales by lambda; the actual gate circuit uses two qubits including the environment. This is a valid alternative channel but not the two physical rails in Khan et al. (arXiv:2604.25429). Published construction: positive and negative parts encode two occupancy qubits with probabilities max(±d,0)/B; each rail is amplitude-damped with survival abs(lambda), then the rails swap for negative lambda. Each damping dilation is a controlled RY and a reverse-direction CNOT, with a fresh environment initialized to zero. Four qubits total for a mode, two controlled RY and two CX, plus SWAP for overrelaxation. The exact reduced density matrix is obtained by tracing the environments; no postselection. Both circuits preserve the trace and reproduce `d * lambda**t` in the decoded expectation. This is **only the scalar relaxation stage**, not full nonlinear BGK/MRT or a proof that the two encodings can be composed with the amplitude transport without readout. Preparation and moment transforms are not counted. Finite-shot sampling of final output probabilities is seeded ideal simulation, not a hardware shot run. The two-rail model has lower variance on these sign-split inputs, while costing more physical rails/environment. At lambda=0.5, d=0.4, B=0.5 and four steps, the target 0.025 is small compared with the branch rail's fixed noise scale. Exact trace preservation does not imply zero shot cost.

## Validation
`run.py` executes 16 transport-only timesteps on each of two dense complex statevectors (4^3 cells, 47 fluid, 17 solid), validates all amplitudes and inverse; then executes a 32-step small hybrid test using classical matched-density CAC and BGK/Guo forcing based on the audited equations, neutral wall extension for derivatives, and quantum-gate transport with exact amplitude encoding and reconstruction. A separate push-loop computes independent streaming, sharing the classical collision routine, so agreement validates transport, not the collision physics. The legacy comparison isolates the source branch's old transport logic, holding initial conditions and classical collision fixed, including zeroing solid populations. It is not a complete reproduction of the remote benchmark. The first legacy step loses about 5.668 of 47 hydrodynamic mass units; it becomes unstable later. Zero solid populations alone does not demonstrate impermeability. Corrected 32-step mass drift is at most 9.24e-14, phase drift 6.99e-15, and reference max population error 9.27e-16. The phase variable is signed, not water volume. The VTK exports raw moment velocity without half-force correction and marks solids, which have zero stored population. Periodic outer domain, internal floor/obstacle, no gravity, equal reference density, manufactured smooth phase, no calibrated wall contact angle. Not a validated waterfall, Laplace benchmark, or full quantum coupled solver.

`channel.csv` records 400 repeated relaxation updates; `shots.csv` records 12 finite-shot parameter combinations, each repeated 256 times. `history.npz`, `hybrid.csv`, `transport.csv`, `report.json` retain numerical evidence. `benchmark.png` and `phase_slices.png` are generated from these arrays. All output generation is reproducible by `bash milestone18/reproduce.sh`; `--full-regression` includes all historical tests. The two previous coherent phase-field and D2Q25 architectures remain separate.
